title: "The Girl and the Hookah"  
author: "Waheeda Khan"  
---


Fifteen-year-old Shanaya was the only child of doting parents. Pampered, indulged, and adored, she had grown up believing that if she wanted something badly enough, she would eventually get it.

She had just completed her Class 10 examinations and now stood at the first major crossroads of her life. The stream she chose in Class 11 would shape her future education; and perhaps her entire career.

Shanaya's heart was set on Arts. She loved literature, stories, and the freedom that came with the humanities. But her father had very different dreams. He wanted his daughter to pursue Science, convinced it would open doors to a more secure future.

Shanaya, however, had no interest in Science.

She was bubbly, adventurous, fiercely independent, and just rebellious enough to know how to twist her parents around her little finger. Being their only child, she usually got her way.

But this time, her father refused to give in.

After days of arguments and negotiations, Shanaya finally placed a strange condition before him.

*"Fine,"* she said. *"I'll take Science... but only if you buy me a hookah".*

A hookah!

Just imagine!

Not a diamond necklace. Not a gold bracelet. Not the latest smartphone. Not designer clothes, expensive makeup, or some exotic vacation.

Out of everything the world had to offer, fifteen-year-old Shanaya wanted just one thing _

***A hookah!***

But why this strange fascination with a hookah; an age-old instrument used to smoke tobacco, a relic that had all but disappeared from modern life?

Shanaya's father silently cursed the day he had narrated stories about his own father, who would sit for hours with a hookah by his side. He had described it as a familiar sight in villages of a bygone era; a ritual that many elderly men considered a form of relaxation after a hard day's work.

He had never imagined that those casual stories would plant a seed in his daughter's impressionable mind.

Now he knew exactly what she was doing.

She was holding him to ransom.

Knowing how stubborn Shanaya could be, he kept postponing the discussion, hoping against hope that she would eventually agree to take Science without insisting on her bizarre condition. But this was Shanaya _ the one and only Shanaya; always determined to do something different, always a step ahead of her classmates when it came to daring ideas.

At school, some of her friends often teased her for being too *"well-behaved."* They laughed because she refused to join them when they secretly experimented with cigarettes.

Although she never gave in to their pressure, their taunts lingered in her mind.

***The real addiction wasn't to tobacco.***

***It was to admiration. To standing apart. To being remembered.***

Her restless spirit wasn't searching for nicotine.

It was searching for distinction.

If they thought smoking a cigarette made them look bold and grown-up, then she would outdo them all.

They could puff away on tiny cigarettes.

Shanaya would smoke a hookah like an emperor from another age.

She imagined the gasps, the whispers, the stunned expressions.

For a fifteen-year-old desperate to be noticed, the spectacle mattered far more than the smoke.

The last dates for admission to Class 11 were fast approaching, yet Shanaya refused to budge an inch. Despite her parents' heartfelt pleas, she stood firm on her peculiar demand.

At last, exhausted by the endless battle, her father surrendered.

After an unusually long search, he managed to find a hookah and presented it to his triumphant daughter. As if that wasn't enough, the poor, unsuspecting father even found himself explaining how to assemble the contraption and how it was traditionally used.

Shanaya had won.

True to his word, her father enrolled her in the Science stream.

With a heart full of excitement, Shanaya walked into Class 11 believing she had struck the perfect bargain. She had Science, she had her hookah, and she had an opportunity to experiment more with life!

She had no idea where the road ahead would take her.

All she knew was that she wanted to live every moment on her own terms, chase every thrill, and write her own story.

Whether that story would become a triumph or a tragedy was a question for another day.

For now, she was simply Shanaya—

***She is not just the girl who asked for a hookah. She is the girl with the hookah.***
